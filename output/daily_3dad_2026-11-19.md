### 1. Embedding Predictive Adaptation and Refinement for Multi-Modal Industrial Anomaly Detection

- **arXiv**: [2611.09521v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09521v1)
- **提交日期**: 2026-11-06
- **分类**: cs.CV
- **Comment**: Accepted to WACV 2027
- **摘要**: Industrial Anomaly Detection (IAD) has evolved from 2D image-based approaches to multi-modal methods that leverage the complementary nature of RGB images and 3D point clouds. Among these, memory bank-based methods, which identify anomalies by the discrepancy between each query patch feature and its nearest normal prototype, have proven particularly effective. However, two limitations remain: (i) employing frozen pretrained encoders yields insufficient discrimination between normal and anomalous features due to the domain gap, while existing contrastive learning-based adaptation struggles to capture the spatial relationships required for precise localization; and (ii) pixel-level anomaly maps interpolated from patch-level distance scores remain coarse, lacking fine-grained detail. To address these limitations, we propose EPAR (Embedding Predictive Adaptation and Refinement). We adapt encoders by predicting the embeddings of masked regions to capture spatial relationships, together with patch-level Variance-Invariance-Covariance (VIC) regularization, resolving the poor discriminability between normal and anomalous features caused by the domain gap. We further propose a Noise Suppression Network that refines coarse maps by learning the relational discrepancy between each patch feature and its nearest normal prototype, rather than the anomaly's appearance. This relational signal remains valid for unseen real defects, despite being learned from synthetic anomalies. EPAR demonstrates strong practical applicability, excelling in few-shot settings and improving AUPRO@1% on MVTec 3D-AD and Eyecandies by +1.8% and +1.9%, respectively.

### 2. Autonomous 3D Visual-Thermal Inspection for Utility-Scale Solar Farms

- **提交日期**: 2026-10-13
- **分类**: cs.AI, cs.CV, cs.RO
- **Comment**: Accepted at the 2026 Asia-Pacific Solar Research Conference (APSRC 2026), Canberra, Australia, 1-3 December 2026
- **摘要**: Utility-scale photovoltaic (PV) inspection is dominated by aerial surveys, which are effective for identifying module-level hot cells but provide limited visibility of critical components located under the array, including connectors, cabling, fasteners and structural elements. These assets are difficult to inspect consistently at scale, and their defects are often spatially small, partially occluded and difficult to localise from isolated images. We present an automated 3D visual-thermal inspection system (Tricorder) for under-panel PV asset inspection. The approach combines LiDAR-inertial Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) with geometrically calibrated RGB and thermal imagery and machine-learning-based object detection and segmentation. We demonstrate the approach on utility-scale PV data for MC4 connector detection and connection-state recognition, thermal anomaly identification, and detection of structural components including bolts, nuts and torque tubes. The results demonstrate a pathway from image-based inspection toward spatially grounded autonomous inspection, where defects are detected and linked to specific assets and their 3D locations within the solar farm.

### 3. Towards Generalizable 3D Anomaly Detection via Relational Inconsistency Modeling

- **arXiv**: [2609.35059v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35059v2)
- **提交日期**: 2026-09-28
- **分类**: cs.CV
- **Comment**: Accepted by NeurIPS 2026. Code: https://github.com/VisualScienceLab-KHU/GRIM
- **摘要**: 3D anomaly detection (3DAD) aims to identify defective regions in point cloud data, serving as a critical component in industrial inspection systems. Existing methods are normality-centered -- learning the distribution of normal samples and treating deviations as anomalies -- without explicitly modeling what constitutes a defect. This leads to ambiguous decision boundaries with increased false positives and negatives, particularly in unified and cross-domain settings where diverse normal distributions further blur the boundaries. We propose a relational inconsistency modeling framework that characterizes defects as violations of geometric consistency among neighboring structures. Our approach learns category-agnostic defect cues through pseudo-anomalies designed as controlled relational violations, instantiated by two key modules: Edge-aware Graph Refinement (EGR) for encoding geometric relationships among local regions, and Cluster-Deviation Modeling (CDM) for identifying regions that are relationally incompatible within their structural peer group. Extensive experiments on Anomaly-ShapeNet and Real3D-AD demonstrate consistent improvements over prior state-of-the-art methods in both in-domain and cross-domain settings, validating the effectiveness of learning an explicit, relation-based defect criterion for 3D anomaly detection. ### 4. PADFormer: Pose-agnostic Anomaly Detection from Sparse View Images

- **arXiv**: [2608.04210v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04210v1)
- **提交日期**: 2026-08-04
- **分类**: cs.CV
- **Comment**: Accepted to ECCV 2026 (oral)
- **摘要**: Pose-agnostic Anomaly Detection (PAD) remains challenging as anomalies can appear under arbitrary viewpoints, requiring methods to handle significant pose variations. Existing approaches rely on complex 3D reconstruction, which are computationally expensive and require extensive multi-view data. We propose PADFormer, a novel image-space approach that leverages Vision Transformer (ViT) to directly reconstruct anomaly-free versions of query images while preserving pose information. Our key insight is to adapt cross-view masked reconstruction for anomaly detection through training exclusively on normal data, combined with dynamic patch selection and spatial alignment mechanisms that enable effective learning from sparse reference views under significant pose variations. Anomalies are detected by comparing these reconstructions with the query image. PADFormer achieves state-of-the-art results on the PAD benchmark while maintaining comparable performance on classic few-shot anomaly detection (FSAD) tasks, demonstrating superior efficiency and generalization without requiring 3D reconstruction.
